SpectArk 1.2.0

A new backup engine, NAS timelines, and a disk that never fills up

  • Every change protected within seconds, a restore point at most every 15 minutes. Only the files that changed are copied, found through the macOS file-system journal — including changes made while SpectArk was not running. Like Time Machine, the last 24 hours are kept whole, then one restore point per day for a month and one per week after that. Your existing backups carry over on the first run.
  • A full backup disk makes room by deleting its oldest backups. Every backup disk keeps 5% free (or what you set in "Keep free space"); when it runs short, the oldest restore points on it go first, across all the backups there. A backup that runs out of room carries on instead of failing again and again.
  • NAS backups show their timeline and restore in the app, give space back on the NAS as old versions are thinned, and never write more than the share can hold.
  • Destinations are found wherever macOS mounts them — a share remounted as "home-1" or a drive back as "Backup 1" resumes on its own.
  • Encrypted backups are incremental and thinned like the others, and restore each file's modification time.
  • Open at login: after a restart SpectArk starts quietly in the menu bar and realtime backup simply continues.
  • Skip rebuildable files (on by default): dependency folders, build outputs and caches are left out when the tool that owns them is recognized.

Also fixed

  • A file changed while it was being copied is never recorded half-written.
  • Realtime backup no longer runs non-stop on developer folders, and no change is left un-backed-up.
  • A file locked in Finder no longer stops a backup; NAS backups no longer stop for good after a crash.
